Cathode Ray Tube

A cathode ray tube (CRT) is an evacuated glass tube in which an electron gun produces a beam of electrons, accelerates it with a high voltage, and steers it onto a phosphor-coated screen. Where the beam strikes, the phosphor glows. Sweeping the beam across the screen rapidly builds up an image.

How it works

  • Emission. A heated cathode releases electrons by thermionic emission.
  • Acceleration. Electrons pass through a potential difference $V_a$ and gain kinetic energy. For $V_a$ well below about 50 kV, the nonrelativistic result is accurate:
    • $$eV_a = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 \quad\Rightarrow\quad v = \sqrt{\frac{2eV_a}{m}}$$
  • Deflection. Deflection plates apply an electric field, or coils apply a magnetic field, to bend the beam.
  • Display. The beam hits the phosphor, which converts kinetic energy into light.

    • Magnetic deflection. A beam of speed $v$ crossing a uniform field $B$ perpendicular to its velocity feels a force $F = evB$. This force is always perpendicular to the velocity, so the electrons follow a circular arc of radius

    $$r = \frac{mv}{eB}.$$

    Electric deflection. Between parallel plates, the field $E = V_d/d$ exerts a constant sideways force $eE$. This gives a parabolic path, as in projectile motion.

    Worked example

    An electron gun uses $V_a = 2.0\ \text{kV}$. Find the speed, then the radius of curvature in a field $B = 1.0\ \text{mT}$.

    Speed:

    $$v=\sqrt{\frac{2(1.60\times10^{-19})(2000)}{9.11\times10^{-31}}}\approx 2.65\times10^{7}\ \text{m/s}$$

    This is about 9% of the speed of light, so the nonrelativistic formula is acceptable.

    Radius:

    $$r=\frac{(9.11\times10^{-31})(2.65\times10^{7})}{(1.60\times10^{-19})(1.0\times10^{-3})}\approx 0.15\ \text{m}$$

    Problem-solving application

    Problem. In the same tube, electric deflection plates of length $L = 4.0\ \text{cm}$ are separated by $d = 1.0\ \text{cm}$ and carry $V_d = 50\ \text{V}$. Find the sideways displacement of the beam as it leaves the plates.

    Solution. The electrons keep their forward speed $v$, so the time between the plates is $t = L/v$. The sideways acceleration is $a = eV_d/(md)$. The displacement is

    $$y = \tfrac{1}{2}at^2 = \frac{eV_d L^2}{2mdv^2}.$$

    Substitute $mv^2 = 2eV_a$ from the acceleration step:

    $$y = \frac{V_d L^2}{4dV_a}$$

    The charge and mass cancel, so the result depends only on voltages and geometry. Numerically,

    $$y=\frac{(50)(0.040)^2}{4(0.010)(2000)}=1.0\times10^{-3}\ \text{m} = 1.0\ \text{mm}.$$

    Design insight. Deflection is proportional to $V_d/V_a$. A higher accelerating voltage gives a brighter, faster beam, but the beam is harder to steer. Designers therefore use long plates or deflection coils placed far from the screen. A small angle at the plates then becomes a large displacement at the screen.
