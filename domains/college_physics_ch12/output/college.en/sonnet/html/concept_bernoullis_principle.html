Bernoulli's Principle

Definition. Bernoulli's principle expresses conservation of energy for a steady, incompressible, non-viscous fluid flowing along a streamline. At constant height, the sum of pressure and kinetic energy per unit volume is the same at every point:

$$P_1 + \tfrac{1}{2}\rho v_1^2 = P_2 + \tfrac{1}{2}\rho v_2^2$$

Here $P$ is the pressure (Pa), $\rho$ is the fluid density (kg/m³), and $v$ is the speed (m/s). Where the fluid moves faster, the pressure is lower. Where it moves slower, the pressure is higher.

The term $\tfrac{1}{2}\rho v^2$ is kinetic energy per unit volume. Since the total is fixed, any gain in kinetic energy must be paid for by a loss in pressure. The equation applies only if the flow is steady, the fluid is incompressible, viscosity is negligible, and both points lie on the same streamline.

To find the speeds, we use the continuity relation $A_1 v_1 = A_2 v_2$ that you already know. A fluid speeds up where the pipe narrows.

Worked example. Water ($\rho = 1000\ \text{kg/m}^3$) flows through a horizontal pipe. In the wide section the cross-sectional area is $A_1 = 20\ \text{cm}^2$, the speed is $v_1 = 2.0\ \text{m/s}$, and the pressure is $P_1 = 150\ \text{kPa}$. The pipe narrows to $A_2 = 10\ \text{cm}^2$. Find $P_2$.

  • Use continuity to find the speed in the narrow section:
    • $$v_2 = \frac{A_1}{A_2}v_1 = 2(2.0) = 4.0\ \text{m/s}$$
  • Rearrange Bernoulli's equation for $P_2$:
    • $$P_2 = P_1 + \tfrac{1}{2}\rho\left(v_1^2 - v_2^2\right)$$
  • Substitute:
    • $$P_2 = 150\,000 + \tfrac{1}{2}(1000)(4 - 16) = 150\,000 - 6000 = 144\,000\ \text{Pa}$$

    The pressure drops by 6 kPa in the narrow section, where the water moves faster.
