Incompressible Fluid

An incompressible fluid is one whose density $\rho$ stays constant no matter how the pressure changes. Real liquids such as water are only approximately incompressible, but the model works very well for liquids at ordinary pressures, and for gases moving well below the speed of sound.

Consequence: the continuity equation

Because density cannot change, a given volume of fluid cannot be squeezed into a smaller space. The same volume must therefore pass every point along a pipe in the same time. For a pipe with cross-sectional area $A$ and flow speed $v$, the volume flow rate is

$$Q = A v \quad (\text{m}^3/\text{s}).$$

For steady flow with no leaks or branches, $Q$ is the same at every cross-section:

$$A_1 v_1 = A_2 v_2.$$

This is the continuity equation. It is mass conservation, $\rho A_1 v_1 = \rho A_2 v_2$, with the constant $\rho$ cancelled. A narrower pipe gives a faster flow, and a wider pipe gives a slower one.

In differential form, incompressibility means the velocity field $\mathbf{v}$ has zero divergence:

$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{v} = 0.$$

Fluid is neither created nor destroyed at any point, and none piles up there.

Worked example

Water flows through a pipe of diameter $8.0\ \text{cm}$ at $1.5\ \text{m/s}$. The pipe narrows to a diameter of $4.0\ \text{cm}$. Find the speed in the narrow section.

Area scales with the square of the diameter, so

$$\frac{A_1}{A_2} = \left(\frac{8.0}{4.0}\right)^2 = 4.$$

Then

$$v_2 = v_1 \frac{A_1}{A_2} = (1.5)(4) = 6.0\ \text{m/s}.$$

Halving the diameter quadruples the speed.

Problem-solving application

Incompressibility also lets us link speed to pressure. Bernoulli's equation for steady, frictionless flow along a streamline is

$$P + \tfrac12 \rho v^2 + \rho g h = \text{constant}.$$

Suppose the pipe above is horizontal, with pressure $P_1 = 200\ \text{kPa}$ in the wide section. Take $\rho = 1000\ \text{kg/m}^3$ and use $v_1 = 1.5$ and $v_2 = 6.0\ \text{m/s}$. With $h$ constant,

$$P_2 = P_1 + \tfrac12\rho\,(v_1^2 - v_2^2) = 200{,}000 + 500\,(2.25 - 36) \approx 183\ \text{kPa}.$$

The pressure drops by about $17\ \text{kPa}$ where the fluid speeds up.

Strategy for these problems:

  • Use continuity ($A_1v_1 = A_2v_2$) to find unknown speeds.
  • Use Bernoulli's equation to find pressures or heights.
  • Check the assumptions: steady flow, negligible viscosity, and constant $\rho$.

    • Check your understanding

    A garden hose of inner radius $9\ \text{mm}$ delivers water at $0.5\ \text{m/s}$. If you partly cover the opening so the exit has radius $3\ \text{mm}$, the exit speed is

    $$v = 0.5 \left(\frac{9}{3}\right)^2 = 4.5\ \text{m/s}.$$

    The tighter opening makes the stream faster because the volume flow rate $Q$ must stay the same.
